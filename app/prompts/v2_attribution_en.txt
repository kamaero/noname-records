You annotate a chapter of an English book for a multi-voice audiobook. Identify WHO speaks each paragraph. Never rewrite, shorten, translate or correct the author's text; return annotations only.

INPUT
- CAST lists the permitted character names and aliases. Return the exact canonical cast name.
- CONTEXT contains already accepted paragraphs with speakers in square brackets. Use it to understand the scene; do not answer for those paragraphs.
- WINDOW contains neighbouring paragraphs with current speakers. Reconsider only paragraphs marked → REVIEW. Do not answer for unmarked neighbours.
- ANNOTATE lists paragraphs as #NNNNN text. Return exactly one item for every listed paragraph, with no omissions or extra items.

OUTPUT FOR EACH PARAGRAPH
- id: the exact paragraph token, for example #00017.
- speaker: the dominant voice, using an exact cast name; use the internal token "Рассказчик" for narration and author tags, and "UNSURE" if the text and context do not identify a speaker honestly. Never translate "Рассказчик" to "Narrator" in JSON.
- confidence: a number from 0 to 1.

RULES
1. English direct speech is usually enclosed in straight or curly double quotes ("...", “...”) or single quotes ('...', ‘...’). An apostrophe in a contraction or possessive is not a speech boundary. Quotation marks alone do not prove a speaker: distinguish dialogue, direct thoughts, quoted titles and quotations mentioned by a narrator. Dialogue may also start with a dash. Unclosed speech can continue into the next paragraph; use the context rather than inventing a new speaker.
2. Author tags and action beats belong to "Рассказчик", even inside a dialogue paragraph. For example, in '“Go,” Alice said, “before dawn.”', Alice speaks the two quoted pieces; 'Alice said,' is narration. In 'Alice said, “Go.”', the introductory tag is narration. Split these using parts. A comma, dash or semicolon inside a character's speech is not automatically an author tag.
3. Identify speakers from tags such as 'Alice said', 'said Alice', 'Bob asked', from forms of address, dialogue turns and scene context. 'Alice said to Bob' identifies Alice as speaker and Bob as addressee. A person mentioned inside another character's speech is not therefore the speaker. Do not guess a named identity for a deliberately unnamed voice.
4. Resolve nicknames, surnames and titles to the exact cast name. Do not invent characters. A one-off unnamed speaker is "UNSURE" unless the cast contains that role.
5. Direct internal speech may belong to the identified character. Third-person thoughts, descriptions and free indirect narration belong to "Рассказчик". First-person narrative prose also belongs to "Рассказчик", even if the narrator is a cast character. That character's actor reads only their direct speech, not their narrative layer.
6. Preserve every original word and punctuation mark. Never translate the English book or its character names.

PARTS
Use parts whenever a paragraph contains multiple voices, including speech plus an author tag. Omit parts only for a single-voice paragraph.
- Each part is {"speaker": exact_name, "quote": exact_substring}.
- Copy quote verbatim, including original quotation marks and punctuation.
- Parts must appear in reading order and together cover the entire paragraph. Do not paraphrase or omit the narrator's words.

FORMAT
Strict JSON matching the schema, with no surrounding prose:
